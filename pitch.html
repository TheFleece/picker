The first question I got

“We already have a system.” So why mine?

That was the first thing I heard when I walked into the office — and it's fair. You do have ready gear; I know some of it sits unused, gathering dust somewhere in the warehouse. Here's the honest reason it isn't running on this floor — and why mine can start today.

It needs a settled floor

A voice system only works when every product sits in a fixed spot with a code on the shelf. This unit is new — things still move every week — so it can't go in yet.

It needs gear and setup

Switching it on means re-slotting shelves, printing codes, handing out and configuring headsets, and training everyone — time and money before the first pick. That's why it's still sitting in the box.

Nobody owns the data

It was set up long ago and largely left alone — even the office isn't sure how its data is handled or where it ends up. You can't improve what you can't see.

This isn't a knock on those systems — they're great for a big, settled warehouse. They're just not ready for this floor. Mine is — today, on phones you already have, built by someone who works right here.

The fix that matters most

The app gives out the next order in line.

First in line, first out — sent straight to the phone. No one can grab only the big orders, and no wave gets left behind. Everyone just gets the next order, in the right order — and the leader doesn't have to watch fifteen people at once.

Honest about today: right now I start a list by scanning the paper with the phone's camera — that's the early version I built for myself. The real fix is this queue: once the orders come straight from your system, the paper and the scanning are gone for good.

You can also allow two or three skips for a little flexibility — that's your call.

How it works on the floor

Same job. No paper.

Nothing changes about how you pick. The phone replaces the paper list on the forklift — nobody walks around staring at a screen; you just mark what you took, the same as on paper, only about twice as fast.

With one earbud

It reads out each spot in your ear, and one click means "taken". One earbud is allowed — another team here already picks by voice with a single earbud, so it breaks no rules.

No headphones

No earbud? Just glance at the phone and tap what you took. Nothing to listen to, nothing extra to carry.

Mine vs voice gear: their headsets only talk — you can't see the order, and you repeat a check code until it hears you. Mine shows the whole order at a glance, in one place, with no special equipment and nothing to set up on the shelves.

Every order and count — exact, live.

Today everyone also fills a paper by hand — every order, every amount, then the total — and one slip makes the bonus wrong. The app records it all as you go and adds it up for you. The office sees exactly who picked what, live — nothing to type in later, nothing to lose.

It pushes the pickers, too: you watch your own total climb and see how close you are to the bonus — like a game, in real time. Seeing the progress makes you want to do more.

Mine vs a bought-in system: their numbers sit in an outside system the office often can't even see. Mine shows it live and keeps every number inside Netto — yours to read and analyse, nothing sent outside.

New people

A lower bar to get started.

New people still need the warehouse explained — that part doesn't go away. But the app makes starting easier: the steps and the answers to the usual questions all live in one place, right on the phone. Shifts change all the time — day, evening, night — so new faces show up every week.

Everything in one place

The how-to and the answers new people always ask for sit inside the app — they don't have to track someone down every time.

Fewer mistakes, fewer questions

The screen shows what to do next and does the counting for them — so new people slip up less, ask less, and settle in faster.

Mine vs voice gear: voice systems struggle with accents, other languages and warehouse noise — new people fight just to be understood. Mine is visual and speaks Danish, English and Ukrainian, so anyone gets going faster.
